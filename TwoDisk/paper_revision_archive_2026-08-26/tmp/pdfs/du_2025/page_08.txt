III. RESULTS
A. Crossﬂow vortices mode
At Re = 2 × 105,Y i met al. [6] observed that the majority of the rotating-disk boundary layer
remains in a laminar state, except near the shroud where a spiral waves structure with azimuthal
wave number k = 82 appears due to strong shear and centrifugal effects by the shroud. In Fig.4(a),
we present the axial velocity w contour plot of the rotating-disk boundary layer under the same
conditions, where w ranges from −0.01 (blue) to 0.01 (red). In all the contour plots characterizing
the boundary layer, the rotation direction of rotating disk is counterclockwise.
The results exhibit a similar behavior to Yim et al.’s [6] ﬁndings, with a uniform occurrence of
k = 75 spiral waves structures in the azimuthal direction. It is noteworthy that the axial velocity,
w, is much smaller than both the radial and azimuthal velocities, and approaches zero within the
boundary layer. Therefore, it serves as an excellent reference for disturbances in the rotating-disk
boundary layer. Consequently, in the contour plots presented in this study, the colors represent the
axial velocity w without any additional transformations.
