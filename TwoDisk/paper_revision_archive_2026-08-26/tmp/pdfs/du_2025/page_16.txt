Since the modes with 10 ⩽ k ⩽ 20 ultimately control the transition to turbulence, this raises another
important question regarding their origin. Are the 10 ⩽ k ⩽ 20 modes excited by mode k = 32? Or
does the energy from the decaying mode k = 32 gradually transfer to the 10 ⩽ k ⩽ 20 modes?
Understanding the mechanism behind this energy transfer or excitation is vital for a comprehensive
understanding of the transition process in the rotating-disk boundary layer.
In the previous section, after the stationary modek = 32 excited by the roughness elements, new
modes appeared at higher radii, which we refer to based on their azimuthal wave number as modes
10 ⩽ k ⩽ 20. In fact, these modes are in relative motion with respect to the rotating disk, as opposed
to the stationary mode k = 32. Therefore, they can more formally be referred to as traveling spiral
waves mode.
