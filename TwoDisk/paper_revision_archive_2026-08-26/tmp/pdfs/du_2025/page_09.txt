In this section, the focus is on discussing the crossﬂow vortices mode (stationary spiral waves
mode) induced by roughness elements. Therefore, Figs. 4(b) and 4(c) depict the axial velocity w
contour maps of the boundary layer on the rotating disk for Case 2 and Case 3, respectively [where
w ranges from −0.05 (blue) to 0.05 (red)]. In comparison to Case 2 where the roughness elements
have a drag coefﬁcient of S = 10, Case 3 features roughness elements with a drag coefﬁcient of
S = 100. As a result, the features of the spiral waves are more pronounced in the contour map
corresponding to Case 3, indicating a stronger inﬂuence of the roughness elements on the ﬂow.
These 32 roughness elements are uniformly distributed at a radial position of r = 6. In Fig. 4(b),
the axial velocity w distribution reﬂects the presence of 32 spiral waves induced by the roughness
elements. However, these spiral waves appear to be discontinuous. Downstream of the roughness
elements, there is a noticeable gradual attenuation of the spiral waves. As the spiral waves convect
downstream, they gradually strengthen, then undergo another attenuation and reach a maximum
energy level at the periphery of the rotating disk. In Fig. 4(c), the spiral waves exhibit a gradual
increase in radial wavelength as they convect downstream. This phenomenon reﬂects the gradual
ampliﬁcation of perturbation energy within the boundary layer.
