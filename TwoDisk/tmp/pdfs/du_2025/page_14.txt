Among all the Fourier modes, the mode k = 32 exhibits the highest kinetic energy. When
S = 100, the kinetic energy for k = 32 is only marginally greater than that for S = 10, suggesting
that the spiral waves in both cases have reached saturation. However, the energy of the harmonic
mode k = 64 for S = 100 is signiﬁcantly greater than that for S = 10, indicating that, despite the
saturation of the spiral waves, the nonlinear effects associated with the k = 32 spiral waves mode
are more pronounced when S = 100 compared to S = 10. Apart from the mode k = 32 and its
harmonics, modes k = 13, k = 14, k = 15, and k = 16 also exhibit high kinetic energies, which
may correspond to the irregular spiral waves observed in Fig. 12. Between k = 80 and k = 120,
the modal kinetic energy exhibits ﬂuctuations, which are associated with the disturbance near
the shroud, are edge-driven, akin to the k = 75 spiral waves structure shown without roughness
elements in Fig. 4(a).
